## 2. What the system is, in plain words

Two sleeves that bet on different things, with two safety switches on top. Paper account, equity about $112k, live under design 070 since 2026-10-29 13:04 UTC.

- **Momentum sleeve, 60 % of the investable book.** Holds SPMO, an ETF of the S&P 500's strongest recent performers, rebalanced by the index twice a year. The return engine; no parameters to overfit. Today it holds 27.5 % of equity and fills toward its 57 % target as the old dip positions exit.
- **Dip sleeve, 40 %.** The dip rule — buy a name that closes 8 % or more below its 90-day high while above its 150-day SMA; −12 % GTC stop-limit on the broker, +20 % target, 60-trading-day holding limit; up to 3 slots of 10 % of equity; the rest of the sleeve in SPY — **now on the 11 SPDR sector ETFs** (Decision 070, deployed today). The operator's 15 hand-picked names are the `shadow_watchlist`: fetched, curated monthly by Claude, backtested at every checkup with `backtest-mech --shadow`, never traded. Why: any single-name list loses with this rule (the Dow 30 lost 8 pp/yr in both halves, 19 of 20 random 30-name S&P lists lost), the hand-picked list's +4 is 2022-onward only, and the sector ETFs are the one hindsight-free universe where the rule is ≥ 0 — they are what every expected number was computed on.
- **Transition in progress.** DXCM, JPM and URI were bought under the old list at the old 25 % size (≈ 67 % of equity together). They keep their HOLD items, stops (68.91 / 297.37 / 940.20), targets and clocks, and exit on their own — at most 60 trading days — never force-sold (a watchlist edit no longer orphan-closes a mechanical position). Until one exits there are no free slots, so no ETF entry can happen. The first execute under the new book ran 2026-10-29 14:15 UTC: clean, three HOLD, nothing orphaned, no SPY (target 0 while the overlay exceeds the sleeve).
- **Insurance.** SPY below its 200-day SMA and fewer than half of the 11 sector ETFs above theirs → exposure ×0.5, no new entries. Era-dependent: costs ≈ 1.7 pp/yr in a bull decade, pays 15–20 points in a 1929 / 1973 / 2008.
- **Shock trigger.** SPY's 10-session return ≤ −7 % on any of the last 20 sessions → exposure ×0.5. Cuts the worst drawdown by about a third at near-zero cost, 80 of 81 settings agree.
- **Exposure = min(insurance, shock)**, never the product; both core legs scale, held dip positions keep their exits.
- **Eight risk gates** before any entry; the correlation gate does not apply to sector ETFs (they are baskets; it would have blocked one second slot in five). Every exit is a cancel-settle-close that never leaves a position bare.
- **Decision 069's go/stop rule**: behaviour (every action rule-prescribed; the fill-vs-backtest ledger's median gap reported), drawdown (never further than SPY in any ≥ 10 % SPY drawdown, ≤ 0.7 × SPY's in any ≥ 20 %), return reported with its 90 % band and **never judged under 3 years** (the luck meter: a true +1.4 pp/yr needs ~170 years to reach t = 2; a quarter fails the old "≥ SPY − 2 pp" test 43 % of the time for a book exactly as good as the backtest; the old drawdown leg failed the backtest 3 times in 5).

Hindsight-free expectation: +1.4 pp/yr over SPY in a decade like 2017–2026 (both halves positive, worst relative year −11), +3.6 over 1927–2026 with all four quarter-centuries ahead, worst drawdown ≈ −20 % vs SPY's −34 %. The return edge is a century claim (t = 3.16, bootstrap +1.9 … +5.2); the decade alone is a coin flip (t = 0.25); the drawdown behaviour is the better-evidenced half. Known cost: the rebound year after a crash (−8 to −11 relative).

**Live since today and still unconfigured**: a heartbeat ping after each daily summary (`HEALTHCHECK_PING_URL` not yet set — the operator's sign-up) and a T-bill leg for idle cash (`cash_ticker`, off; only for a live account that pays nothing on cash). ## 4. The discipline — how every result in this project was produced

Hypotheses in the script docstring before anything runs; marginal effects before leaderboards; a grid finds a family, never a winner. Walk the *selection* forward both ways (decade split 2022-01-01; century split 1975 for books, 1990 for universe tests; four quarter-century eras), and report through 2025-12-31 beside the full period because 2026 dominates every momentum number. Any single-name universe needs the point-in-time membership mask. Judge against SPY **and** the equal-weight hold of the universe; the 11 sector ETFs are the hindsight-free floor. De-risked capital earns 0 % in the engine (a deliberate bias against de-risking rules; `carry.py` is the honest-expectation line with real bill rates). Costs 0.05 % a side, 1 %/yr borrow on shorts, price a short as the broker would. **Every state-, label- or timing-based idea gets a placebo** with the same count and shape; a cell with one to three episodes is an anecdote. Anything that survives gets the luck meter (`luck.py`: deflated Sharpe, PBO, block bootstrap).

Lessons added today (`docs/insights.md`): a placebo that beats *both* the candidate and the incumbent is a verdict on the era, not the signal (member breadth); a short list is not a quality list; a mirror rule can only act where the original binds (the shock release had nothing to release because the insurance was binding in every decade rebound); a consensus by out-of-sample rank alone picks the cells the second halves liked — demand both halves on every universe; a quarter cannot judge a return edge. **Data rule**: every Alpaca file in `data/param_search/alpaca_hist/` is dividend-adjusted (`adjustment=all`); a split-only fetch shifted SPY's reference from +1.36 to +2.27 before it was caught — never mix; `historical_long/SPY.json` ends 2026-10-12 on purpose (restore it from the server archive if it is ever touched).
